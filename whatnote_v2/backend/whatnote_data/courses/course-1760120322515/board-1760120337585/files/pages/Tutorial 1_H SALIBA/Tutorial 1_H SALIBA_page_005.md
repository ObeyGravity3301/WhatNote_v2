5- Koch’s postulates  
Robert Koch was a famous scientist of the XIXth Century who established the bacterial 
etiology of anthrax and tuberculosis.  He formulated four postulates that are used to 
establish the etiologic relationship between any identified microbe and a given disease.  
According to these postulates, the identified organism must: 
1- Be present in all cases of the disease and absent in all individuals who do not 
present that disease 
2- Be isolated from diseased patients  
3- Cause disease when transmitted to a healthy individual (natural infection) or 
inoculated in an experimental animal 
4- Then be isolated again from the new host  
 
Questions:  
 
- Later on, Koch himself rejected the first postulate.  Can you think of a given 
infection situation where this postulate cannot be applied?  
- For many decades, viral infections presented a technical problem when it came 
to apply the second postulate.  Why?  
- The third postulate is not always true.  Can you think of a given infection 
situation where this postulate cannot be applied?
