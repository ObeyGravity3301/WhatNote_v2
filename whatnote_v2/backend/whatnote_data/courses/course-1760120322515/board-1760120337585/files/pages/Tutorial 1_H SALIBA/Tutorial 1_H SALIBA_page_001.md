I- Historical overview  
 
1- Redi’s experiment :  
The theory of spontaneous generation was prevalent until the Middle ages. 
According to this theory, living organisms, such as flies, maggots, microbes, and even 
rodents, can, under some circumstances, spontaneously develop from non-living 
matter.  For example, maggots (fly larvae) can develop from rotting meat.  In 1668, 
Redi performed an experiment that challenged this theory.   
 
He placed a fresh piece of meat in each of 3 jars. Jar #1 was left open, jar #2 was 
tightly sealed and jar #3 was covered with a porous gauze.  The experiment is
